Diabetes can reduce sensation (neuropathy) and blood flow in the feet, so small injuries can become serious infections.
Daily: look at both feet, including between the toes, using a mirror or a family member if needed. Wash and dry carefully. Moisturize the skin but not between the toes. Never walk barefoot, including at home and at the mosque. Check shoes for stones before wearing them.
A foot examination by a health professional is recommended at least once a year, and more often if there is neuropathy or a previous ulcer.
Seek same-day care for a wound that is red, warm, swollen, painful, has pus, or a bad smell, or for black skin. Seek emergency care if there is fever with a foot wound. Do not treat corns or calluses with sharp tools or chemical removers.
