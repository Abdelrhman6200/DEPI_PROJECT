Continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) reports several standard metrics over the last 14 days.
Time in range (TIR) is the percentage of readings between 70 and 180 mg/dL. For most adults with type 1 or type 2 diabetes the common target is more than 70 percent. Each 10 percent increase in TIR is meaningful.
Time below range (TBR): below 70 mg/dL should usually be less than 4 percent, and below 54 mg/dL less than 1 percent. For older or high-risk adults, targets are often more cautious: TIR above 50 percent and time below 70 under 1 percent.
Time above range (TAR): above 180 mg/dL should usually be less than 25 percent, and above 250 mg/dL less than 5 percent.
The Glucose Management Indicator (GMI) estimates the HbA1c expected from the average CGM glucose. It can differ from a laboratory HbA1c.
Coefficient of variation (CV) measures glucose variability; 36 percent or lower is generally considered stable.
At least 70 percent of possible CGM data over 14 days is recommended before drawing conclusions. Targets are individualized by the clinician.
