Carbohydrates raise blood glucose the most. Common Egyptian high-carbohydrate foods include baladi bread (about 50 g carbohydrate per loaf), white rice (about 45 g per cooked cup), koshari (often 90 g or more per plate), feteer, potatoes, dates, sugarcane juice, and sweets such as basbousa, konafa, and om ali.
Helpful swaps: half a loaf of bread or whole-wheat (sen) bread instead of a full loaf; freekeh instead of white rice; ful medames and lentils as protein-rich carbohydrate; tea without sugar; whole fruit such as guava or orange instead of juice.
The plate method: half the plate non-starchy vegetables (salad, molokhia, okra, green beans), a quarter protein (grilled chicken, fish, eggs, white cheese, kofta), and a quarter carbohydrate.
Spreading carbohydrates across the day and walking for 10-15 minutes after meals can reduce the rise in glucose after eating. Individual responses differ, so checking glucose before and 1-2 hours after a meal helps people learn their own patterns.
