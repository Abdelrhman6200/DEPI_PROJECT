title: Low blood sugar (hypoglycemia): recognise and treat

Hypoglycemia means a blood glucose below 70 mg/dL. A level below 54 mg/dL is clinically significant and needs prompt action.
Common symptoms are shaking, sweating, a fast heartbeat, hunger, anxiety, blurred vision, headache, and confusion. Some people, especially older adults or those taking beta blockers, may have fewer warning signs.
The 15-15 rule: if you can swallow safely, take 15 grams of fast-acting carbohydrate, for example 3 teaspoons of sugar dissolved in water, half a cup of juice, or 3-4 glucose tablets. Recheck after 15 minutes. If glucose is still below 70 mg/dL, repeat. When glucose is back above 70, eat a snack or meal if the next meal is more than an hour away.
Never give food or drink to someone who is unconscious or cannot swallow. Call emergency services (123 in Egypt). Glucagon can be given by a trained family member if prescribed.
Medicines that can cause hypoglycemia include insulin and sulfonylureas such as gliclazide and glimepiride. Skipping meals, more activity than usual, alcohol, and reduced kidney function increase the risk.
Repeated lows, especially at night, should always be reported to the care team because the treatment plan may need review. Patients should not change medicine doses on their own.
