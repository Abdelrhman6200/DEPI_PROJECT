title: Metformin and kidney function

Metformin is a first-line medicine for type 2 diabetes. It does not usually cause hypoglycemia on its own.
Kidney function (eGFR) should be checked at least yearly while taking metformin, and more often when eGFR is below 60. Metformin is generally not used when eGFR is below 30. Between 30 and 45, it is usually not started, and the prescriber reviews the dose of existing treatment.
Common side effects are stomach upset and diarrhea, which often improve with the extended-release form and taking it with meals. Long-term use can lower vitamin B12, so B12 may be checked periodically, especially with neuropathy.
Metformin may need to be paused temporarily for some imaging with contrast dye and during dehydrating illness, according to the care team's instructions.
