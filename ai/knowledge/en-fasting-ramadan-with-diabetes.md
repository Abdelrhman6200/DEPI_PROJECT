---
title: Fasting Ramadan with diabetes
source: Prototype KB - summary of common Ramadan diabetes education

Many people with diabetes wish to fast during Ramadan. The decision should be made with the care team 6-8 weeks before Ramadan, based on an individual risk assessment. People with frequent hypoglycemia, recent severe low or high glucose, advanced kidney disease, pregnancy, or older adults with frailty are usually considered high risk.
Medicine timing and doses often need adjustment before Ramadan, especially insulin and sulfonylureas. This must be done by the prescriber.
Check glucose regularly during the fast; checking does not break the fast. The fast should be broken immediately if glucose falls below 70 mg/dL, rises above 300 mg/dL, or if there are symptoms of hypoglycemia, dehydration, or acute illness.
At suhoor, prefer slowly absorbed foods (ful, oats, whole-wheat bread, eggs, yogurt) and eat as late as possible. At iftar, break the fast with water and 1-2 dates, then a balanced meal, and limit sweets and sugary drinks.
