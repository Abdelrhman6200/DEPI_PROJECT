Illness, infection, steroids, and stress can raise blood glucose. During illness, check glucose more often (for example every 4 hours), keep drinking water, and keep eating small amounts of carbohydrate if possible.
Some medicines may need to be paused temporarily during dehydrating illness (vomiting, diarrhea, fever), commonly metformin, SGLT2 inhibitors (such as empagliflozin and dapagliflozin), ACE inhibitors, ARBs, diuretics, and NSAIDs. This must follow a plan agreed with the care team; patients should not stop or change medicines without advice.
Seek urgent care if glucose stays above 300 mg/dL, if you cannot keep fluids down, if you have vomiting with abdominal pain, fast breathing, drowsiness, or confusion. People taking SGLT2 inhibitors can develop ketoacidosis even when glucose is not very high, so these symptoms always need urgent assessment.
